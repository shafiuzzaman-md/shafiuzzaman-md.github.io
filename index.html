Bio. I am a Ph.D. candidate in Computer Science at the University of California, Santa Barbara (UCSB), expected to graduate in Spring 2027, advised by Prof. Tevfik Bultan. I work as a Graduate Student Researcher in the Verification Lab (VLab), supported by DARPA’s HARDEN and INGOTS programs. Before my Ph.D., I built large-scale software systems in industry.

Research. I work at the intersection of AI-assisted software engineering, software security, program analysis, and formal methods. My dissertation builds toward one pipeline that can find, confirm, and repair security vulnerabilities in real code. Its centerpiece, SAILOR, pairs static analysis with LLM-orchestrated symbolic execution and sanitizer-backed validation, and has uncovered 67 confirmed vulnerabilities. PatchAnalyzer then assesses whether a patch really fixes them. This builds on STASE, which is being transitioned into U.S. Army adversarial-testing workflows. PatchAnalyzer already gives exact guarantees about patch correctness under explicit assumptions, and I am extending that approach to other security properties.

Security Findings

Responsible disclosure

More than 30 security vulnerabilities, each confirmed, disclosed across 18 widely used C and C++ codebases, including post-quantum cryptography, speech and audio models, and security-policy tooling. Each one is confirmed by a sanitizer crash, and each entry on the findings page links to the maintainers' own advisory, issue, or fix. These come from SAILOR's 67 confirmed vulnerabilities across 28 projects. Some are already fixed upstream, including in SELinux and mruby, and others are still in coordinated disclosure.
